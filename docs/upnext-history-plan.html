peeq · implementation plan · 2026-07-25

Queue and Activity become Up next and History

Six separable pieces. Piece 1 is the UI change itself; piece 2 is a few lines of backend with the largest felt improvement; pieces 3–5 remove the remaining waiting and make a download explain itself; piece 6 is flagged and deliberately not built.

Context

peeq's /activity page stacks two unrelated things — a log of what already happened above a projection of what's coming — under one filter row that only half-applies to each. Meanwhile /queue shows work in flight on a separate page in a completely different visual language. So "what is peeq doing" is split across two pages, and neither answers it on its own.

The move: Activity splits into two rail items, and Queue is absorbed into the future half. The rail keeps nine items. Design and mockups: docs/ sibling artifact, published at the URL in the session notes.

Two lanes, not one. Downloads are serialised deliberately (one worker, one at a time, so YouTube doesn't block us). Summaries run on a separate worker calling the LLM — summarizeWorker.Run at backend/cmd/peeq/main.go:405 — and never touch yt-dlp. So a download and a summary routinely run at the same time: at most two jobs, one per lane. The page is built on that. Making them share one lane is a real option but it is a change to peeq, not to this design.

1 · Rail split and the two pages

frontend+ one backend fixships alone

Almost entirely frontend — it reuses the existing props, endpoints and SSE events. One backend change is unavoidable, described under "The schedule section" below.

Rail

  • ui/src/shell/Rail.tsx — in SECTIONS, Collect becomes Add · Inbox · Up next · History. Remove the queue item, replace activity with two items. ViewId gains upnext and history, loses queue and activity.
  • Icons from the existing ui/src/icons.tsx lucide map — a clock-forward glyph for Up next, a history/rewind glyph for History. No new icon dependency.
  • Fix the stale comment at index.css:284 ("the rail only holds seven items") while touching the file — it already said nine.

The pill

Show a count on Up next only while a video download or summary is running; the number is running + waiting across both lanes. Housekeeping (scans, metadata, retention, yt-dlp checks) never counts. History never has a pill.

  • This is today's queueCount (App.tsx:508-512) plus one gate: render nothing unless at least one job is in state running in either lane. No backend call is needed — Job.state and SummaryJob.state are both already on the props App holds, so the gate is jobs.some(j => j.state === "running") || summaries.some(s => s.state === "running").
  • Keep the undefined-until-both-loads-settle behaviour exactly — unknown is not empty.
  • Stated assumption: a running summary alone lights the pill, same as a running download. That follows from queueCount already summing both.
  • Consequence to eyeball once live: while everything is paused nothing is running, so the pill disappears with items still waiting. That is the rule as specified; the pause banner is the louder signal.

Routing

  • ui/src/route.ts — add upnext → /up-next and history → /history in both parsePath and toPath.
  • Legacy paths /queue → upnext and /activity → history, following the existing case "pending": case "decide": pattern at route.ts:102-104, so normalize rewrites the address bar. Update the scope comment at route.ts:8.
  • Decided: /activity points at History. It currently means "everything", but its content is a log, and a log is what a bookmark to that URL wanted.

State ownership — do not move the subscriptions

App owns every subscription today and both new views are pure renderers of props. Keep it that way. The SSE hub is memoryless — "a client that subscribes only sees events published after it subscribed" (sse.go:107-109) — so if a view owned its own subscription, navigating away from Up next and back would lose an in-flight download's progress until the next tick. Today that cannot happen because Queue.tsx holds no state at all; the split must preserve that.

  • Stays in App: the progress and summary SSE streams, the 3-second poll that retires completed downloads (there is no job-finished event), refreshQueue, refreshSummaries, and the liveActivity buffer.
  • History receives the fetched log plus liveActivity and keeps today's merge-by-id, the before= pagination and the "+N earlier" edge — moved across from Activity.tsx unchanged.
  • Up next receives jobs, progressByJobId, summaries, summaryPhaseByVideoId, onCancel, onOpenChannel — Queue's six props exactly — plus the filtered upcoming list.

Views

  • New ui/src/views/UpNext.tsx. Two tiers, and they are headed differently on purpose: the lanes are named after the kind of work ("Downloading", "Summarising"), each leading with its running job as a hero followed by its own waiting line; the schedule below is named after time ("Within the hour", "Later today", "This week"). Kind-then-time, because the lanes are about what is happening and the schedule is about when. A lane with nothing running and nothing waiting renders no header at all.
  • Waiting rows read "then" in the lead column, not a rank. With two independent lanes a number would invite a comparison that doesn't exist — a waiting summary does not hold up a download. Row order carries position without claiming a rank that isn't real.
  • New ui/src/views/History.tsx — the grouped timeline: mono time gutter, day separators, outcome on the node ring, kind leading the detail line as a past-tense word. Largely today's Activity.tsx agenda markup with the "Up next" section removed.
  • Delete ui/src/views/Queue.tsx and ui/src/views/Activity.tsx. App.tsx's ViewSwitch (~line 673-757) routes the two new views; Queue's six props move to UpNext unchanged, Activity's to History.
  • No page titles on either page, per the rest of peeq. History's retention line moves to the end of the filter chip row.

The schedule section — the one backend fix

GET /api/activity/upcoming projects pending downloads and summaries as approx: true items (activity_handlers.go:103-118) — the very jobs the lanes now show from the jobs and summaries props. Rendering both would print every waiting download twice.

  • The schedule renders only items with an exact at (approx === false) — peeq's own timed housekeeping. The approx ones are already in the lanes, with better data.
  • Filtering client-side is not enough. The ordered items are added first and share one upcomingCap of 20, and the handler's own comment notes they "sort ahead of every timed one in Merge". So with 20+ pending downloads the endpoint returns no timed items at all — the schedule section would silently vanish exactly when the queue is busy. The fix has to be server-side: give ordered and timed items separate budgets, or let the caller ask for timed only.
  • Cheapest honest version: since no consumer will want the ordered items after this change, stop projecting them and let the cap apply to timed items alone. Confirm no other caller depends on them first.

2 · The pacer waits in the wrong place

backendindependent

Nothing has touched YouTube for two hours, you click download, and peeq waits 20–35 seconds anyway. Paste a URL and it is 40–70 seconds, because the details lookup is its own throttled call. backend/internal/ytdlp/ytdlp.go:261 states it outright:

// Every call waits at least its own gap, however idle the Runner is.
slot := now.Add(gap)

The gap is applied leading — in front of every call — rather than trailing, between one call and the next. The comment's justification does not hold: a call that already fired pushed nextSlot to its own finish plus a gap, so taking the later of now and nextSlot already guarantees full spacing. The leading term protects nothing and only adds latency.

Shipped as PR #157, all four CI checks green. The one-liner below was subtly wrong for the interactive lane and the implementation corrected it — see the note under the snippet.
- slot := now.Add(gap)
+ slot := now
+ if slot.Before(r.nextSlot) {                       // busy: something ran within a gap
+     if isInteractive(ctx) {
+         slot = now.Add(gap)                        // jump the queue, but clear what just started
+         if slot.Before(r.nextInteractiveSlot) { slot = r.nextInteractiveSlot }
+     } else {
+         slot = r.nextSlot
+     }
+ }
  tail := slot.Add(gap)                             // unchanged: the next call still waits a full gap
  • The interactive lane must keep now+gap when busy. The symmetric change — max(now, nextInteractiveSlot) — would be a bug: nextInteractiveSlot tracks only the priority lane, so a background call that started at or just before now is invisible to it, and a click arriving at that instant would fire on top of a call already in flight. Two yt-dlp processes hitting YouTube together is the exact failure the pacer exists to prevent. Idle, it still goes at once.
  • defaultSleep needs hardening. A zero wait is newly reachable, and select over an already-fired timer and an already-cancelled context picks at random — a cancelled caller would proceed about half the time. Check ctx.Err() before arming the timer.
  • Cold start. With nothing recorded, the first call after a restart fires instantly. Fine normally, but a crash-looping peeq would then hit YouTube every boot — consider stamping the tail once at startup.
  • defaultSleep already returns immediately on a non-positive duration, so no guard is needed for slot == now.
  • These existing tests encode the leading gap and will fail — expect it, don't be surprised by it. In backend/internal/ytdlp/ytdlp_test.go: TestThrottle_idleRunnerWaitsExactlyOneGap (769) and TestThrottle_interactiveStillWaitsItsOwnGap (846) assert exactly the behaviour being removed and must be inverted — an idle Runner should now wait nothing. TestMetadata_throttle_sleepsWithinBounds (77), TestThrottle_floorAlwaysAtLeast20Seconds (109), TestThrottle_jitterAddsRandomComponent (138), TestThrottle_defaultJitterAppliedWhenUnset (170) and TestThrottle_appliesInAnonymousMode (384) all measure the sleep of a single call on a fresh Runner; each needs a preceding call so the gap it measures is the trailing one.
  • Must stay green unchanged: TestThrottle_spacesOutConcurrentCallers (714), TestThrottle_interactiveSkipsTheBackgroundQueue (799), TestThrottle_backgroundQueuesBehindAnInteractiveJump (878) and TestThrottle_cancelledContextReturnsPromptly (189). They assert spacing and ordering, which this change preserves — if any of them break, the change is wrong.
  • New: an idle Runner fires immediately; a Runner idle past its tail fires immediately on both lanes.
Related, and it will get noticed after this lands: the 10-minute watchdog is armed before the pacer wait (download/worker.go:405-408), so a pure queueing delay can surface as "watchdog timeout: no progress". This change makes that far less likely rather than fixing it. Out of scope; worth a follow-up issue.

3 · What a download is actually doing

backend+ frontendafter 1 and 2

A download job has no notion of a phase anywhere — not in ytdlp.Progress, not in download_jobs, not in the progress SSE event. So the bar sits at nothing through the whole preamble, then pins at 100% and hangs through the merge.

Most of this is free. execWithProgress passes every stdout line to onLine (download.go:273) and the regex drops all but [download]. yt-dlp's own [youtube] Extracting URL, [info] Downloading 1 format(s), [Merger], [SponsorBlock] and [SubtitlesConvertor] lines already arrive and are discarded. No new yt-dlp flags, no schema migration.
  • Add Phase to ytdlp.Progress; add matchers alongside progressLineRe for the lines above; include phase in the progress SSE payload (main.go:280-291).
  • Synthesize the two pre-phases, which have no process to emit them: waiting on the pacer and looking up the video. throttle already computes the exact start instant, so "starts in 34s" is a real number, not a spinner — surface it rather than a generic wait.
  • Runner.Download currently reports only through onProgress func(Progress); widen it or add a second callback to carry a phase before cmd.Start().
  • Label the two passes. peeq requests best-video + best-audio, so yt-dlp runs 0→100% twice, and the thumbnail and subtitle fetches print their own 100% lines. All currently feed one unlabelled bar, which is why it visibly resets mid-download and looks like a restart. Naming the pass explains it.
  • Verify against a real download first. backend/internal/ytdlp/testdata/fake-ytdlp.sh emits a single sweep, so nothing in CI covers the double sweep. Capture real output before writing the matchers, and extend the fixture to match.
  • Cancel stays available through every phase except the last — once files are being joined and moved, stopping halfway leaves debris.

4 · A person's download should not queue behind a robot's

backendafter 2

Approving in the Inbox is a person clicking, but the download worker never marks its context WithInteractive, so an approved video queues behind a channel scan that happened to start first. After piece 2 that is the remaining avoidable wait.

The distinction already exists in the data — no schema change. Every user-initiated enqueue uses downloadPriority = 10 (downloads_handlers.go:19, used by the Inbox approve, the re-download button and the channel handler); the scan scheduler uses autoPriority = 0 (scan/scheduler.go:34). The worker can simply mark the context interactive when job.Priority > autoPriority.
  • This deliberately contradicts the WithInteractive doc comment ("never for worker calls"). That comment is right about scan-driven downloads and wrong about approved ones — update it to say so rather than leaving the next reader to think the rule was broken by accident.
  • The trade-off to state plainly: the interactive lane keeps its own tail, so approving ten videos at once puts ten jobs in the priority lane, and a subsequent click — adding a channel, pasting a URL — then waits behind all ten. Today that click goes first.
  • Recommended: take the simple version and watch for it. Bulk-approving ten videos and immediately pasting a URL is a rare shape, and the fix if it bites is a third tier (true clicks ahead of approved downloads ahead of scans), which is more machinery than the problem currently justifies.
  • Test: a priority-10 job jumps ahead of a queued scan; a priority-0 job does not; a burst of approvals does not starve the background lane entirely.

5 · Stop fetching the same video twice

backendafter 2

A pasted URL runs yt-dlp twice: once for the metadata preflight (download/worker.go:346-378, only when video.Title == ""), then again for the download. Two YouTube calls, two pacer gaps, two extractions — for one video, seconds apart.

  • The preflight already fetches the full info JSON via -J --skip-download. Persist it to the job's staging directory and hand it to the download with --load-info-json, which skips extraction entirely.
  • Expiry is not the risk here. Format URLs live several hours and the download follows within seconds. The real risks are that --load-info-json may still make network calls depending on yt-dlp version, and that a stale info JSON from a retried job could be reused — key the file to the job and delete it on failure.
  • Scope note: this only helps the URL-added path, because channel-scan videos already have a title and skip the preflight. That is exactly the path this is about.
  • Verify empirically before committing to it: run both variants against a real video and compare wall-clock and the yt-dlp output lines. If --load-info-json still contacts YouTube, the second gap remains and the change is not worth its risk.

6 · Flagged, not built

deferred
  • Skipping a scheduled item. Filed as issue #156 rather than built — cancel on scheduled rows is a new capability, not part of a move.
  • Watchdog covering the pacer wait. The 10-minute watchdog is armed before the throttle sleep, so a pure queueing delay can report as a download failure. Piece 2 makes it far less likely without fixing it.
  • Merging the two lanes. If downloads and summaries should share one lane, that is a change to peeq's execution model, not to this UI.

Sequencing

2 first — a handful of lines, no UI risk, and the biggest felt improvement. Then 1, which is the actual feature and is independent of everything else. Then 4 and 5, which each remove another avoidable wait and are independent of one another; 5 should be measured before it is committed to.

3 last. It depends on 1 for somewhere to render, and it reads far better after 2, 4 and 5 — otherwise most of the detail it works to surface is peeq explaining a wait that shouldn't have existed.

Each piece is its own branch and PR off a worktree. Pieces 2, 4 and 5 all touch the pacer or the download worker, so land them in sequence rather than in parallel to keep the diffs readable.
