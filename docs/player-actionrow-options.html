The line from Keep forever to the category pill

Six controls, four different heights, two hairlines and one pill stranded at the far end. Below: the row as it ships, five ways to settle it, and every one of them redrawn at iPhone width — which is where the wrapping problems actually show up. Nothing here is scaled or stylised; the rows are at their real pixel sizes in the app's own colours.

The sleep bug is already fixed

The sleep menu was a plain absolutely-positioned child of its pill, pinned top: calc(100% + 7px) with no idea where the viewport ended. On an iPad the action row sits about 100px above the fold, so six presets opened straight off the bottom of the screen.

Now: a shared useMenuPlacement measures once per open and flips the menu upward when there is more room above than below. The category picker and the ⋮ menu got the same treatment — they had the identical flaw, so fixing only the one you hit would have left two behind. .sleepmenu also gained the height clamp .catmenu has carried all along. This is independent of everything below: whichever option you pick, the fix survives it.

Five ways out

01

One height, one silhouette

smallest change

Change nothing about what the row contains. Give every control the same 40px height and the same rounded shape, move the subtitles icon to sit between Sleep and the ⋮, drop the second hairline, and push the category pill to the right edge so its distance reads as intended rather than leftover.

01 · 1024px wide

What changes

  • Every control is 40px tall with a 20px radius — one family, one baseline.
  • Subtitles moves right of Sleep, so the row ends on the two icon controls rather than splitting them around a pill.
  • The second hairline goes; margin-left: auto does that job better.
  • No control appears or disappears, and no behaviour changes at all.

What it costs

  • watch .metapill is shared with Library cards, Decide and Channels. The override must be scoped .playacts .metapill or the whole grid moves with it.
  • The row gets taller, not shorter: the pills grow 30px → 40px and the kebab 32px → 40px. Still under Apple's 44pt guidance, but the right direction for the device this came from.
  • It still reads as six loose things. This tidies the mess; it does not resolve it.
02

What it is, then what this sitting does

Give the row a rule you can say in one sentence. On the left, the controls that change the video permanently — kept, watched, filed. On the right, pushed to the edge, the ones that only affect this sitting: sleep, subtitles, and the overflow. Both hairlines disappear because the gap now carries the split.

02 · 1024px wide

What changes

  • The category pill joins the two toggles — all three say what the video is, and all three outlive the page.
  • Sleep, subtitles and ⋮ move to the right edge as one cluster, in that order.
  • Both hairlines go. The gap between the clusters is the divider.
  • Shown with the timer armed, so you can see the countdown pill at its widest — the row must not reflow when it arms.

What it costs

  • The category pill jumps left. It is documented at Player.tsx:1219 as belonging with the actions "behind a separator" — that comment gets rewritten, not ignored.
  • Same scoped-.metapill caution as 01.
  • This is the option that suffers most on a phone: once the row wraps, margin-left: auto stops meaning anything and the rule becomes invisible.

It stays fully editable — it is a button up there too. What is left below is five controls and one hairline.

What changes

  • The category becomes an 11px pill on the byline, one chevron wide, still a real picker.
  • The action row drops to five controls and one hairline, ending on the ⋮ — which is where an eye expects a row of actions to end.
  • It is also the option that survives a phone best, because it is the shortest row.

What it costs

  • argue first This directly reverses the reasoning at Player.tsx:1219–1222. The counter-argument: editable does not mean action. The Library card's watched chip is editable and lives on the card, not in a button row.
  • An 11px chevron is a small tap target. It needs padding beyond its ink to clear ~40px on touch.
  • The byline already ellipsises long channel names and wraps at narrow widths; a fourth item makes that busier.
04

One toolbar, not six controls

Put everything inside a single bordered shell with internal dividers. Instead of six objects that each need their own edge, there is one object with segments — and the segments can then carry the grouping that the two floating hairlines were straining to. The chrome moves from the controls to the container, so the controls go flat and quiet.

04 · 1024px wide

What changes

  • Three segments, each a sentence: what this video is (kept, watched, filed), what this sitting does (sleep, subtitles), and everything else (⋮).
  • The ⋮ gets its own segment. It is not a sibling of the category — it is the lid on six more actions, and a divider is the honest way to say so.
  • Every control loses its own background and border. State is carried by colour alone.
  • The category stops being an outlier: it is simply part of the first segment.

What it costs

  • The biggest new pattern here — nothing else in Peeq is a segmented toolbar, so it arrives without precedent to lean on.
  • Flat controls inside a box read as less tappable. Hover and pressed states have to work harder, and there is no rest-state border hinting at the hit area.
  • A fixed shell cannot wrap. Below roughly 620px it has to break into stacked shells — see the phone section below, where this is the only option that needs new rules rather than just tolerating a wrap.
04b

…with subtitles split off

You asked whether subtitles needs its own divider next to Sleep. Here is that version, side by side with 04 above, so the difference is visible rather than argued.

04b · 1024px wide

The case for it

  • A 40px bare icon sitting flush against a 30px pill reads as unrelated whatever the grouping says. The divider stops them looking like one broken control.
  • They are genuinely different verbs: sleep schedules something, subtitles toggles something. Only their scope is shared.

The case against

  • Four segments for six controls is barely a grouping any more — it is back to a divider between every pair, which is the thing 04 was fixing.
  • The looseness you are reacting to is a sizing problem, not a grouping one. Giving subtitles a 34px boxed hit area to match the pill fixes it without the extra rule.
  • Every added divider is width the shell cannot spare on a phone.
05

Two decisions and a menu

Only two things in that row are decisions you make often: keep it, and mark it watched. Leave those labelled and let everything else fall into the ⋮ menu that already exists — subtitles and sleep included, sleep carrying its countdown as a flag so the armed state stays readable without opening anything.

What changes

  • The row drops to three controls plus the ⋮. Both hairlines are gone with nothing left to separate.
  • Subtitles and Sleep become menu rows with a right-hand flag showing their state — the flag slot RowMenu already has.
  • The quietest of the five by a distance, and the only one that gets calmer rather than merely tidier.

What it costs

  • Subtitles goes from one tap to two, on the control most likely to be toggled mid-video. That is the real price here.
  • An armed sleep timer stops being visible on the page. "Is it still running?" needs a look inside the menu — a countdown badge on the ⋮ would answer it, at the cost of the calm this option is buying.
  • Sleep needs a submenu or an inline preset strip. Neither exists in RowMenu today, so this is more new code than it looks.

04

One toolbar

Breaks — measured 199px of overflow. A single inline-flex shell has no wrap point, so it runs off the right edge rather than folding. It needs a real rule: below 620px, split into two stacked shells — permanent state on top, this-sitting plus ⋮ underneath.

05

Two decisions and a menu

Two easy lines, because there is almost nothing in it. The ⋮ pushed right by margin-left: auto lands beside the category rather than alone, so it avoids 01's stranding by luck — at a slightly different width it would not.

What the phone pass actually decided. These are measured, not eyeballed: at 322px of usable row, today's row takes three lines, 01 and 02 also take three, 05 takes two, 03 takes two, and 04 overflows its container by 199px. So 04 needs a structural rule (two stacked shells below 620px), 01 and 02 need a one-liner each to stop margin-left: auto stranding a lone control, and 03 needs nothing at all. If a phone matters as much as the iPad does, 03 wins on that axis outright.

Two rules every option was checked against. Nothing here rests invisible until hover — @media (hover: none) is not an escape hatch, since an iPad with a trackpad reports hover: hover. And nothing restyles the shared .metapill or .chip rules unscoped: those selectors are used by the Library grid, Decide, Channels and the share page.

They combine. 03 is about where the category lives and 05 is about how much the row holds; either works with 01's single height underneath it, and 04's shell could hold 03's shortened row. Say which parts you want and I will draw the combination before building anything.
